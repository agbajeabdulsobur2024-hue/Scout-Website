How it works

Ask → Record → Monitor →
Detect change → Alert →
Retrieve → Continue

This model sits underneath every command Scout supports. Once it clicks, the rest of the product makes sense.

01

Tell Scout what matters

You give Scout a market, condition, level, zone, setup, or preference — in plain language or a command.

You say — "watch for a breakout entry on ETH above the range high, stop below 2400"
02

Scout builds the context

Scout structures the request around the relevant market information — turning it into a trade-plan monitor, not a vague note.

Scout does — parses entry, stop, and structure condition into a tracked trade-plan monitor
03

Scout keeps watching

The condition remains monitored instead of disappearing after one interaction. You don't need to ask again.

State — ETH breakout monitor, active and unattended
04

Something changes

The monitored market condition changes — price breaks the range, a zone gets tested, structure shifts.

Event — ETH breaks above range high, displacement confirmed
05

Scout tells you what matters, and why

Scout surfaces the relevant event and explains the context behind it — not just that price moved.

Scout says — "ETH broke above your range high with confirmed displacement. Stop remains below 2400."
After the alert

Retrieve and continue

The loop doesn't end at the alert. You can ask what happened, why, or resume something you paused — days or weeks later, stored in a durable database that survives restarts and new sessions.

You askScout does
"What was I watching?"Retrieves your active watches
"What conditions did I ask you to monitor?"Retrieves the relevant conditions
"Why did you alert me?"Retrieves the recorded event and market context
"Resume this setup"Continues the monitoring workflow — no re-describing it
